## PAGE: About

The Digital Transformation Group works on one question: how can students and staff gain from generative AI without losing the knowledge and judgement that make their work worth trusting?

### What we do
- Run hands-on workshops that build judgement, not just tool skills.
- Publish self-study exercises and guidance for students.
- Develop guidelines for staff on bringing GenAI into teaching and assessment.
- Design and deliver workshops for university staff.
- Turn real staff challenges into student projects and hackathon tracks.

### Our approach
- **Fundamentals first.** AI is most useful when it improves work built on knowledge you already have.
- **Examples over rules.** We teach with worked good and bad examples, not lists of dos and don'ts.
- **People decide.** AI can assist and flag problems. Decisions that affect people stay with people.
- **Responsible by design.** We collect workshop data only with ethics approval and informed consent.

## PAGE: The JUDGE framework

JUDGE is DAIM's framework for professional judgement in an AI-enabled world. In 2026/27 it has three strands.

| Strand | Who it's for | What it involves |
|---|---|---|
| **Graduate Standard** | Undergraduate and taught postgraduate students, across more than one school | Workshops, self-study exercises and assessed evidence of judgement |
| **Essentials** | University staff, including professional services | A workshop on where AI fails in real university work and where people stay in the loop |
| **Applied** | University teams and functions | Real staff challenges turned into student projects |

### The delegation test
Before handing a task to AI, ask three questions:

1. **Consequence:** Does the output affect someone's outcome?
2. **Reversibility:** Would a mistake be hard to undo?
3. **Verifiability:** Can you check it properly in under five minutes?

If the output affects someone's outcome, is hard to reverse, or can't be checked in five minutes, **a person decides.**

### Agentic AI Bootcamp {#agentic-ai-bootcamp}

**[Dates] · [Time] · [Location / online] · Open to all DAIM students**

AI is moving beyond chatbots that answer questions to agents that plan, use tools and carry out multi-step tasks on their own. This bootcamp shows you how these systems work, how to build one, and how to judge whether it can be trusted.

#### What you'll do
- Learn what makes a system "agentic": planning, memory, tool use and multi-step reasoning.
- Build a working agent that uses tools to complete a real task.
- Test it: find where it fails, loops, invents information or takes the wrong action.
- Decide where a person must stay in the loop. The JUDGE delegation test (consequence, reversibility, verifiability) applies even more when AI is taking actions, not just writing text.
- [Optional: Work on a challenge drawn from real university processes]


### Learn by finding the faults
A set of self-study exercises and quizzes. Each one gives you an AI-generated piece of work that looks convincing but contains mistakes, such as:
- a literature review
- a report
- a piece of code
- a slide deck

Your job is to find what's wrong and explain why. You'll also find worked examples of good and poor AI use for each task.

### GenAI in teaching and assessment
Practical guidelines, with examples, for bringing GenAI into your modules.

**In assessment**, AI tools can support checks that take people a long time, for example:
- checking that referencing follows the required format;
- checking that every number in a report matches what the submitted code produces.

**In workshops**, AI can be part of the learning:
- examples of where an AI tool can help solve a workshop task, and where it can't;
- guidance for students on using AI to learn a new concept, not to skip it.

## PAGE: Work with us (JUDGE Applied)

Every team has tasks that are slow, repetitive or frustrating. Through JUDGE Applied, our students work on them.

### How it works
1. **Tell us about a challenge** in your team's day-to-day work.
2. **We turn it into a project brief** for our students.
3. **Students work on it** under academic supervision, as a student project or a hackathon track.
4. **You get the results:** a prototype, an analysis or recommendations, plus a clearer view of where AI could help.

### What we need from you
A short description of the task: what it starts from, the steps your team takes now, what a good result looks like, and who can answer students' questions.
